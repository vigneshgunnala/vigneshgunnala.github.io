Featured research · Independent study

Predicting soil-moisture deficit from weather alone

Sole author Random forest · station-held-out CV Manuscript in preparation

After two years living in Ireland, I noticed how uneven the rainfall is and wondered how farmers decide when to irrigate without soil-moisture sensors. This study asks whether daily soil-moisture deficit can be predicted from rainfall, temperature and wind alone, and how far those predictions hold up at a station the model has never seen.

Main finding. On series with lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.98, a random train–test split makes the model look far better than it is. Holding out whole stations raises MAE from 1.43 to 2.23 mm (56% higher) and RMSE by 58%. The hatched bar above is the number most papers would report.

Practical result. Weather-only predictions land within 0.33 mm MAE of a model with full instrumentation and clearly beat the conventional water-balance baseline. That makes sensor-free irrigation guidance a realistic option for farms without probes.

Leave-one-year-out R² 0.878. Hardest case: Moore Park in the 2018 drought (R² 0.745, MAE 6.90 mm). Autoregressive forms of the target were excluded throughout.
